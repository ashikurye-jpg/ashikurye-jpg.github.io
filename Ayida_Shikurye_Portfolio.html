As-Built

Original transmission schematic and isometric render from the design brief.

3D CAD assembly of the gear transmission, modeled in Autodesk Inventor.

Designed a single-stage gear transmission to transmit 14 hp over a 6,000-hour design life. Applied the Lewis bending-stress equation and DE-Goodman fatigue criteria to size gears, shafts, and bearings, then produced a full 3D assembly and dimensioned 2D manufacturing drawings in Autodesk Inventor — validated with a 3D-printed physical build.

Autodesk Inventor Lewis Bending Equation DE-Goodman Fatigue 3D Printed

Input speed had to step down from 1,500 rpm to roughly 75 rpm while carrying 14 hp, with the input and output shafts in-line and extending 4 in outside the gearbox, a center distance between 5.5–10 in, and an overall shaft length under 16 in. Gears specified in ASTM A602 M8501 steel, shafts in as-rolled 1050 steel, at a 20° pressure angle, sized for a 4,000–8,000-hour (6,000-hour design) service life under light-to-moderate, intermittent loading.

Manufacturability check: final dimensions were reviewed against a real gear manufacturer (Wire EDM Services, Simpsonville, SC) to confirm the design was actually producible — not just theoretically valid.

  • Solved gear-train kinematics to fix tooth counts and pitch diameters, then applied the Lewis bending-stress equation (load, size, and velocity factors k₀, kₘ, kᵥ) to size the face width against the corrected bending-fatigue strength of the gear material.
  • Built 2D free-body diagrams of the countershaft in both the XZ and YZ planes, solved bearing reactions at A and B, and combined them into the resultant bending moment at the pinion (|M| = 1,348.32 lb·in) and transmitted torque (T = 588.24 lb·in).
  • Sized the shaft diameter with the DE-Goodman fatigue design equation, iterating stress-concentration factors for a keyway (K_t bending = 2.7, K_t torsional = 2.43) — first estimate 1.13 in, converged to a standard 1.25 in diameter after the K_f correction pass.
  • Selected a 35 mm-bore radial ball bearing by required dynamic load rating, then checked the shaft shoulder and fillet radius against the bearing's minimum shoulder diameter and maximum fillet radius to confirm a proper seat.
  • Modeled all four gears, both shafts, the keyway, and housing in Autodesk Inventor; produced dimensioned 2D manufacturing drawings for each part and the full assembly.
  • Built a scaled, 3D-printed physical model of the assembled gearbox to confirm the CAD geometry actually assembled and meshed as designed.

The as-built design fixed the pinion at 18 teeth and the gear at 80 teeth to hit a 20:1 reduction. Drag either slider below to see how tooth count drives output speed and torque multiplication — same relations used to size this gearbox.

As-Built

Safety factor simulation across the fully assembled shaft-gear system in Autodesk Inventor.

3D free-body diagram showing applied loads on gears A and B.

Full structural analysis of a nonuniform shaft carrying two gears — 3D free-body diagrams, reaction forces, and bending/torsional/transverse stress at the critical point. Principal stresses solved analytically and via Mohr's Circle, then checked against Maximum Shear Stress and Distortion Energy failure theories. Shaft deflection modeled in MATLAB with singularity functions and validated against an Autodesk Inventor safety-factor simulation.

Mohr's Circle MSS / DET Failure Theory MATLAB FEA Validation

A 1,080 mm nonuniform shaft supported by pillow-block bearings at each end, carrying two gears — a 600 mm gear (A) 400 mm from the left support and a 300 mm gear (B) 750 mm from the left support — with an 11 kN force on gear A at 20° and a reaction-solved force on gear B at 25°.

  • Built 2D and 3D free-body diagrams and solved static equilibrium at both bearings to establish the full external loading condition on the shaft.
  • Constructed torque, bending-moment, and transverse-shear diagrams along the full shaft length, identifying gear B as the critical section.
  • Solved bending normal and torsional shear stress at the critical point, then combined them into the xy-transverse shear state at two candidate points on the cross-section.
  • Solved principal stresses and maximum in-plane shear stress two ways — analytically and graphically via Mohr's Circle — as a cross-check between methods.
  • Modeled shaft slope and deflection with MATLAB singularity functions across the full length, then cross-checked the profile against an Autodesk Inventor FEA simulation (max Inventor deflection ≈ 54 mm near the gear span — both methods agreed on location and shape, differing in magnitude due to Inventor's full 3D geometry vs. simplified beam theory).

The design was originally specified in A36 steel. Running the failure-theory numbers caught a problem before it became a build — toggle between the original and revised material to see why.
